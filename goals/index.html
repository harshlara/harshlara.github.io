Operating Principles

These are the principles I use to guide how I live, learn, and work. They are reminders to myself.

1. Embrace Continuous Learning

Idea: Everything is an iteration.

  • Curiosity first → Learn without pressure for immediate results.
  • Release outcome pressure → Let outcomes guide direction, not weigh you down.
  • Feedback cycle → Do your best, seek feedback, refine, repeat.
  • Conscious iteration → Pay attention to patterns — otherwise, you’ll repeat mistakes on autopilot.
  • Start and end cycles → Begin new learning loops intentionally, and end ones that no longer serve you.

2. Time as a Finite Currency

Idea: You spend time once — make it count.

  • Structure creates freedom → Discipline builds capacity for spontaneity.
  • Time-box efforts → Estimate, execute, and use the feedback to sharpen your intuition.
  • Build time intuition → Regularly study how you use time to improve alignment with your values.
  • Choose with intent → Ask: “Is this the best use of my time, aligned with my long-term vision?”

3. Embrace Discomfort, Build Resilience

Idea: Choose your suffering.

  • Trade-offs are inevitable → Exercise today or regret it tomorrow; learn now or lack the skill later.
  • Push through resistance → Short-term pain precedes long-term reward.
  • Exponential learning → Early effort feels hard, but momentum compounds.
  • Effort vs. outcomes → You control the input; learn to release attachment to the results.

4. Radical Honesty

Idea: Growth starts with the truth.

  • Acknowledge reality → Name both strengths and weaknesses without denial.
  • Self-honesty first → It’s a path to maturity, wisdom, and clarity.
  • With others, add diplomacy → Share honesty at the level people can accept.
  • Why it matters → Honesty builds trust, alignment, and freedom from self-deception.

5. Execution Philosophy

Idea: Bias toward action, always.

  • Avoid analysis paralysis → Overthinking is automatic failure.
  • Start now, optimize later → Momentum matters more than perfect planning.
  • Diversify life → Don’t hinge your identity on one thing — make your life rich and interesting.
  • Mastery takes time → Skills require long-term dedication (~10,000 hours).
  • Opportunities emerge through effort → Consistent learning and action create unexpected openings.

Closing Note

I don’t expect perfection in following these principles — only consistency in returning to them. Each one is a reminder: to learn, to act, to be honest, to endure, and to spend time wisely.
